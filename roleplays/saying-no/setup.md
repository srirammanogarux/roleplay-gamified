# Saying no to last-minute work · Setup

id `saying-no` · game type **Saying no** · Gate 1 draft

## 1. Pitch

It's Friday at 5:45 and you're about to leave for your best friend's birthday dinner. Marcus asks you to do the client forecast over the weekend. Say no, offer something better, and keep him on your side.

- **Marcus**: the boss (same office, same week as the other roleplays).
- **Sarah**: shows up once, in Guided.
- **The player**: a faceless silhouette, never named.
- Off screen: Maya, the friend whose birthday it is. She only appears as a name on the group chat card.

## 2. Situation and facts

Marcus walks over: *"hey, before you head out. i know it's been a long week, but the client wants the revised forecast by monday 9 am. can you take it this weekend?"*

"i know it's been a long week" is the one nod to the other roleplays. It fits whatever order they're played in.

The story gives you these facts. The winning answer uses all three:
1. **The dinner was planned weeks ago.** It's a real reason, so you don't need a fake one.
2. **You stayed late Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday.** You've already done your share this week.
3. **The client meeting is on Wednesday.** That's why "monday by noon" works: it still gives him two days before the meeting.

**Face-off:** `YOU vs MARCUS` · tagline *say no. keep the job.*

**Mission**
- Goal: *Say <span class="hl">no</span> to the weekend, and keep Marcus on your side*
- Stars:
  1. **show you heard him**: say back what he needs and when
  2. **say a clear no**: give the real reason, not a fake excuse
  3. **offer another way**: give him a better plan before he asks for one
- Clock: *your uber is <b>4 minutes</b> away*

## 4. Conversation pieces

**Meter: "how he reads you".** One bar with two ends:

- **pushover** on the left (0), a **green zone** in the middle (35–65), **attitude** on the right (100).
- It starts at **40**, just inside green on the left, because people tend to cave.
- Hedging moves it left: "maybe", "i'll try", saying sorry, fake excuses.
- Getting defensive moves it right: blaming him, sarcasm, "not my problem".
- A kind, clear no and a good alternative pull it back to the middle.
- **You lose at either end.** 0 means you said yes (Gave in). 100 means he's done with you (Got rude).
- It's built on the anger meter with the same faces. Only the bar changes: a green zone in the middle and a loss at both ends.

**Clock: the Uber.** It counts down from 4:00 to when your ride pulls up. The app draws Uber alert cards:

- at 2:00: *Your driver is 2 min away*
- at 0:00: *Your driver has arrived*

When the clock runs out, you have to go, and if you never said no, it counts as a yes. Exit line: *"your ride's here. i'll take that as a yes. file's in your inbox."* This reuses the `meeting-exit` clip (he turns and walks off).

**Surprise moment: the guilt trip.** It fires the first time the player says no, or when the 2:00 Uber card lands if that comes first.

- Marcus glances at your phone: *"go on. your ride's here. i'll just do it myself."*
- The meter is pulled 10 left. This is the test: hold the no kindly, or cave.
- New `guilt-trip` clip (calm to disappointed), caption *I'll do it myself.*

**Sarah's hint** (Guided only, when the meter is near the pushover end)
- Card: `Said no, kindly: the real reason, no excuse`
- Hint: *don't apologise your way into a yes. say no once, give the real reason, and remind him you've stayed late all week.*
- Taking the hint gives star 2 and caps the roleplay at 2 stars.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Stamps on wins, and a face per ending.** Leave's original results screen only stamps fails (red MISSION FAILED) and time's up (amber TIME'S UP). It picks one of three faces based only on fail, win or neutral. Gold, lavender, and a different face for each ending (Gave in should show a pleased Marcus, not a furious one) all need the roleplay file to supply `{ stamp, colour, face }` for each ending, which means a small change to that screen.
   *Recommendation:* make that small change: stamp text, a colour class and a face for each ending, with the layout and animation left as they are. Leave's results stay exactly the same.
2. **The two-ended meter.** The two-ended bar is the heart of "Saying no", but it's new engine work: the anger meter with a green zone and a loss at both ends. The other option is to reuse the anger meter unchanged and make Gave in a scripted loss instead of a meter edge.
   *Recommendation:* build the two-ended bar. It's a small change to the existing meter, and it makes "too soft" as visible as "too harsh".
3. **How the player learns "Wednesday".** The calendar card in scene 3 gives the key fact away plainly. The other option is to make it harder: Marcus mentions it in passing during the conversation.
   *Recommendation:* keep the calendar card plus the narrator line. Leave states its facts just as plainly, and the skill being tested is using the fact, not spotting it.
